### `~/ $ mandate`

I am training to manage capital the way a portfolio manager is accountable for it:
**an explicit objective, a policy allocation, a risk budget, a repeatable research
process, and an attribution report that tells the truth.**

My public work is the evidence trail: portfolio construction and VaR on the Bourse de
Casablanca, a live market-data terminal, full equity valuations rebuilt from the
filings, and a daily stock-pitch discipline. **FMVA® · BIDA® · CFA® track (preparing).**

> and risk research on Moroccan listed equities, and keeping a daily pitch desk on the

## The process I train on

<table>
  <tr>
    <td width="20%" valign="top"><b>01 · Mandate &amp; IPS</b></td>
    <td>Start from the client's objective, constraints, horizon, liquidity needs, and
    tolerance for loss — not from a view. The investment policy statement is the contract
    every later decision is judged against.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td width="20%" valign="top"><b>02 · Strategic allocation</b></td>
    <td>The policy portfolio sets the beta. Equities, fixed income, cash, and alternatives
    sized by horizon, liquidity, and drawdown tolerance — the largest driver of long-run
    outcomes, and the easiest place to fool yourself.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td width="20%" valign="top"><b>03 · Security selection</b></td>
    <td>Bottom-up valuation inside the policy bands: DCF, comparables, fundamental screens,
    and factor exposures. A thesis has to survive the filings before it earns a position —
    and a margin of safety before it earns size.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td width="20%" valign="top"><b>04 · Construction &amp; risk</b></td>
    <td>Covariance, position limits, concentration rules, factor exposures, VaR and CVaR,
    and stress tests. Risk is sized before the return case is made: the first question is
    what breaks the book, not what beats the benchmark.</td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td width="20%" valign="top"><b>05 · Monitoring &amp; attribution</b></td>
    <td>Performance against the policy benchmark, Brinson and factor attribution,
    rebalancing discipline, and written reporting. The record is the product: returns are
    explained, not merely reported.</td>
  </tr>
</table>

> The desk is the process, not the view.

## How I work

<table>
  <tr>
    <td width="50%" valign="top">
      <b>Risk first, return second</b><br/>
      <sub>Every position starts with what hurts me. If the drawdown story holds, the
      upside takes care of itself.</sub>
    </td>
    <td width="50%" valign="top">
      <b>Process over prediction</b><br/>
      <sub>I don't bet on being right — I bet on being able to explain, test, and size a
      view. Faith in process, not forecasts.</sub>
    </td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
    <td width="50%" valign="top">
      <b>The write-up is part of the decision</b><br/>
      <sub>If it cannot be put in front of an investment committee — sourced, sized, and
      falsifiable — it is not a decision yet.</sub>
    </td>
    <td width="50%" valign="top">
      <b>Reproducible research</b><br/>
      <sub>Pipelines over one-off spreadsheets — versioned, schedulable, and honest about
      their confidence. No black boxes in finance.</sub>
    </td>
  </tr>
</table>
